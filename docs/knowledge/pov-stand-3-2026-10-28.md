The session that asks whether FLUX 3 Image Klein draws first-person frames better than Qwen-Image 2.1, on story D of
the owner's own story world. Two cards for two hours: a text card on simple-serving, where Gemma writes the frames'
descriptions and some of the scenes as `internal` work behind the readers, and a picture card that serves Qwen and

## What is drawn

Story D and its world are the owner's module outside the repository, given by its path, `$D` below, which reads the
owner's seed beside it; every command of the stand names it with `--stories $D`. Without `--stories` the
stand runs A and B of [examples/pov-stand.ts](../../examples/pov-stand.ts) as it did before, and this session does not
run them.

[local/pov-stand.ts](../../local/pov-stand.ts) runs the story through the bot itself, for seed 7 and for seed 11 in
each of its ways, of the seeds and writers `run` is given (`--seeds`, `--texts`; this session runs `--seeds 7` alone, so
that nothing of seed 11 is told, described or drawn; each run adds its seeds to `$OUT/seeds.json`, and `check`,
`bundles`, `judge`, `tally` and `review` cover those seeds alone unless given `--seeds`), and keeps each frame's request,
answer, prompt, references and graph as the bot made them (a cell). A way is a writer in a mode:

| Writer | The scene after the reader's input | The frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stand` | the scene written for the stand (`text`) | described by Gemma |
| `gemma` | told by Gemma from the reader's input, as in the bot; `text` is sent nowhere | described by Gemma |
| `claude` | the scene written for the stand | the frame written beside the scene for that mode (`frames`), with no call to the card; the bot takes it as it takes Gemma's answer, through the same parse and the same assembly. A scene with no frame for the mode fails with `no_frame` |

| Mode | |
| --- | --- |
| `pov` | the viewer, the module's `viewer`, is chosen after the opening, and keeps a stub portrait no frame may send |
| `plain` | no viewer is ever chosen, and the viewer keeps no portrait at all |

And at seed 7 alone, the `claude` frames once more in both modes with nobody keeping a portrait but the viewer's stub
(`noref`), so that the bot draws them as it draws a frame without references: its text-to-image graph and its own
prompt without the reference wording. They answer the owner's question of 2026-10-28, whether the references are what
leaves the faces stoic.

A cell's key is the scene, the writer and the mode where they are not `stand` and `pov`, `noref`, and the seed:
`D1-s7`, `D1-gemma-plain-s11`, `D1-claude-plain-noref-s7`. Gemma tells each scene once for a seed, and every mode
takes that scene: the bot's scene does not depend on the picture mode nor on the pictures (local/prompt.ts
`makeRequest`), and a second telling would be one more difference between the modes. The telling is saved under
`tales/`, with its calls, time and usage and a compaction it brought on, a scene whose telling failed as its code; each
cell keeps a copy, and a later run replays it before the cell's frame, so that the story after it is unchanged. A
compaction the bot asks for is answered by the card, saved under `memory/` by its request and replayed for the same
request. The opening stays the stand's.

**The text card's order.** Every request of the story starts with the same system prompt and the owner's seed, about
49000 characters, and differs after it by the scenes told so far; a card that keeps one prefix resident computes again
only what a request does not share with the one before. So `run` asks in an order that keeps one history on the card
from one call to the next, and never more than two calls at once (`IN_FLIGHT`), which leaves the tester's lane its
turn. The `claude` and `noref` runs ask nothing but a compaction and start at once, so that the picture card has
frames from the first minute. Then, seed by seed: Gemma's 36 scenes, told by one run of the bot that draws nothing
(`told` rows in `run.log`), each request the one before with a scene more; then Gemma's frames, both modes side by
side, whose two requests for a scene differ only in the instruction at the end; then the stand's frames the same way.
A priority queue of calls could not do this: each run asks one call at a time and waits on it, so a run's next scene
cannot be asked ahead of its own frame. What it costs: during the telling one call is in flight and the second slot
waits, and a later phase starts only when the one before has ended. Each call's `text_call` row in `run.log` keeps,
beside its input and output, the tokens the card found cached of its input (`cached`, the gateway's
`usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens`, local/serving.ts) and those it wrote anew, the input past them and the
output (`fresh`); either is null where the card did not say. Their sum over the calls is what the stand adds to the
card's copy of its cache, so a second session can be sized by it: the two calls at once stay, with no governor.

Every cell whose frame was described is then drawn at its seed in each of its arms:

| Arm | Checkpoint | Graph | Prompt and references |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `qwen` | Qwen-Image 2.1 | the bot's own, as the bot built it | the bot's |
| `klein` | Klein | `gpu/image-workflow-klein.json`, or `image-workflow-klein-edit.json` when the bot's graph has references | the bot's prompt unchanged, the reference wording included; the same reference files in the same order, the unused slots removed; the bot's canvas |
| `qwen-pose` | Qwen-Image 2.1 | the bot's graph built as the bot builds it, with one reference slot more | the bot's references, then the pose picture; the prompt with one sentence added |
| `klein-pose` | Klein | the edit graph | the same references and prompt as `qwen-pose` |
| `qwen-noref` | Qwen-Image 2.1 | the bot's own text-to-image graph, as the bot built it for a `noref` cell | the bot's, which has no reference wording; no references |
| `klein-noref` | Klein | `gpu/image-workflow-klein.json` | the same prompt; no references |
| `klein-kitchen` | Klein | as `klein`, with ModelAttentionBackend at `comfy kitchen attention` on the sampler's model | as `klein` |

The two pose arms are drawn only for a scene with `pose`, and only on Gemma's cells, in both modes; the `noref` arms
only on the `noref` cells, which no other arm draws; `klein-kitchen` only on the 36 `claude` POV cells of seed 7,
with the `klein` pictures of the same cells as its control. A `noref` cell whose graph has a reference slot is skipped with
`references_in_noref`. The sentence the pose arms add, with n the pose
picture's slot and k the slot of `pose.person` among the bot's references, is `Image n shows a pose only: the person
from image k takes exactly the body pose of the figure in image n; take nothing else from image n.` A frame whose
references do not hold `pose.person` skips both pose arms with `pose_person_not_referenced`, and `qwen-pose` is skipped
with `bot_graph_not_rebuilt` if the build without the pose does not give back the bot's graph byte for byte. Each skip
is a record beside the pictures and counts in `check`.

The pose picture goes up as the bot sends a reader's own picture: stripped (local/reference.ts `strippedReference`),
under a random name in `bot-references` of the temp directory, and blanked after the job with the bot's own blank, as
each of the bot's references is, a failed job's too. Klein draws plainly in every arm but `klein-kitchen`: the bot puts
the kitchen's attention on Qwen's graphs alone (local/picture.ts: `attends` only where the text encoder is
`qwen_image`), and the stand draws the bot's graph as the loopback card caught it, plainly as well. `klein-kitchen`
answers the owner's question of 2026-10-28, whether the attention changes Klein's pictures or its time. It puts the
node on with image-batch.ts `withAttention`, as the bot does on Qwen's, whatever the server offers, and keeps beside
each picture `attention`, what the bot would have logged as its `pictureAttention` there: `kitchen`, `plain_offer`,
`plain_torch` or `plain_unanswered`, by the two reads of local/picture.ts `kitchenAttention`, which the bot does not
export and the stand repeats.

**Order.** The stand draws only while the card's queue is empty and never sends `front`, so the readers' pictures go
first. Of what is described it takes the cheap pictures with a likely gain first, by the owner's rule: Claude's written
frames at seed 7, the controls the next two arms are set against, then the 36 `klein-kitchen` pictures, then the
`noref` frames, then Gemma's and the stand's frames as the text card describes them (and seed 11's after all of seed
7's, in a run that has it). It is a priority, never a wait: a later picture is drawn whenever nothing before it is ready. It keeps one checkpoint on the card for a block of 10 frames (`--block`), all of that checkpoint's arms of each,
and then gives the card the other checkpoint for the same frames. Ten is the trade between two costs: each swap reloads the
other model's weights (Qwen 17.28 GB and Klein 11.02 GB, together about all of a 5090's 32 GB, so they are not
expected to stay on the card side by side; the swap time was not measured), and a stop at `--until` leaves the two
checkpoints at most ten frames apart. When the checkpoint on the card has nothing left to draw, because the describing
is behind, the card takes the other's at once rather than wait: a swap then costs only the minutes the card would have
waited. Each switch is a `block` row in `run.log`, and the first picture after it a `block_first` row with its
milliseconds and those since the switch: if a swap proves slow, a later `run` takes `--block 20`.

**A job on the card.** Each picture goes through the harness's staged path (local/image-batch.ts `stageSocket` and
`submitOnStage`, as local/action-draw.ts draws): one socket for the run, and a prompt id minted before each submit, so
that a submit whose answer is lost is looked for on the card, and its picture is not sent again before the journal
below has closed its id. A dropped connection is waited out for two minutes a job (`OUTAGE`; `--outage-seconds` sets
it, for the dry run). Right before each submit the card's
queue is read, the references go up, and `--until` is asked once more: a job that could not end by it is not sent.
Every request of the drawing ends at `--until`; what stops a job then and deletes its record, and the blanks over its
references, have one minute past it (`CLEANUP_RESERVE_MS`). A job the card answers and will not draw
(`comfy_rejected_prompt`, `comfy_http_error`, `image_failed`) is kept, on every arm, as a record with its code and
milliseconds and is not asked again; any other failure is asked again, three times in all, counted in
`draw-tries.json` so that a run started again goes on counting; `check` counts a picture with its three tries spent as
`exhausted` under its arm, and `run --retry-exhausted` gives those their tries again. A job `--until` cut is stopped,
counts nothing against the picture, and is drawn by the next run.

**The journals.** What may be on the card is written down before it can be there. `submits.jsonl` has each job's
minted id, its key and arm, appended before the submit leaves (the stand reads the id from the request's body), and
`uploads.jsonl` each reference's upload name before it is sent; each gets a row with `closed` once the card is known
to keep nothing of it. An id is closed when the job is out of the card's queue (taken out and interrupted, both by its
id, while it is there), its record deleted by the stand's own `POST /history` and read back as absent from
`/history/<id>`; a drawn job's id is closed beside the next job, any other before its references are blanked. A name
is closed when its blank went through. Every open id and name is closed at the start of a run before its first job
(a `journals` row says how many were open and left), the open ids again every ten seconds while any is, and both at
the end within the reserve. A picture with an open id is not submitted again until that id is closed; a card that
does not answer leaves it open (`submit_open`), the picture waits and the others go on. Before each new job every open
name is blanked first, and no job begins while one is open; a name still open after three such passes while the card
answers stops the run (`unblanked_stop`). Each blank is asked three times, three seconds apart. The run's last row
says `unblanked: N` and `openSubmits: N`.

No pause for the tester's cache (the owner, 2026-10-28).

**The text card's end.** The text card is destroyed once O3 is done, and the picture card draws until its own end.
On `--via direct` a gateway out of reach for two minutes on end, its tunnel closed, ends the describing as
`text_card_gone`; the drawing goes on. With seed 11 off no text call is due that late.

**The disk floor.** Before each draw and before each picture is written, the stand reads the free space of the disk
that holds `$OUT` (`statfs`): under 2 GiB it writes one `disk_low` row with the free bytes, draws nothing more, stops
the text calls, the one on the card included, and ends after its cleanup with exit code 3. A picture the floor stops
is not written and is drawn by the next run; nothing saved is removed. The disk is the one the tester's bot keeps its
database on. `--free-bytes` stands in for the disk's own count in the dry run.

**Exit status.** `run` ends with exit code 1 after `draw_crashed`, `describe_failed`, `missing_on_card` or
`unblanked_stop`, or with an id or a name still open in the journals at its end; with exit code 3 after `disk_low`,
whatever else happened.

**The card.** It is a Klein box, one ComfyUI v0.42.1 on cu130 with Triton serving both checkpoints, as the tester's bot
uses it too. Nothing on the stand's way compares the server's revision with v0.37.0 (that check is in the Qwen
harnesses, `image-identity`, `image-pilot` and the levers, which the stand does not call): every arm takes what the
server reports, writes it once as the `card` row of `run.log` and keeps it with each picture as `pins`
(local/image-batch.ts `serverPins`: ComfyUI's version, torch's, the card and Triton).

**Records.** `cells/<key>.json`; `tales/<scene>-s<seed>.json`, Gemma's scenes; `pictures/<arm>/<key>.png` and, beside it, `<key>.json` with the arm, checkpoint,
seed, milliseconds, file, reference count, the graph sent and the server's pins, and for `klein-kitchen` the attention
verdict, or a refusal's code; `draw-tries.json`, `submits.jsonl` and `uploads.jsonl`. `check` and `describe.json` give
each frame's number of references, never whose they are, each arm's `exhausted` pictures, and the journals' open ids
and names (`open`). Each story's library, which holds its story, is
made under `scratch/` and removed when its run ends, and `scratch/` itself at the next `run` after a crash. A second `run` asks for and draws only what is missing,
arm by arm. `check` counts by way: the POV fields for `pov` cells only; for Gemma's scenes their length in characters
and how many the bot took with their time line; and in every way the cells whose frame holds another number of people
besides the viewer than the scene was written with (`peopleOff`), which stay in every tally and are also counted apart.
Its `told` counts Gemma's scenes and their calls, which no cell counts. For `claude` and `noref` it lists each frame the bot rejected, by key and code, and each it changed on the way to the prompt, by key
and what changed: names cut (`namesStripped`), a person left with no look (`withoutLook`), the viewer taken out of the
people (`viewerInPeople`), more than four people, which nothing cuts (`overFour`), POV fields in a plain frame
(`povFieldsInPlain`).

**Judges.** GPT-6 Astra at `high` sees every drawn picture of a scene without `judge: false`, of every writer, mode
and arm, in bundles of at most 24 pictures of one seed with them all mixed. Each picture comes with the scene's
`intent`, a `plain` one marked `Ordinary picture.` before it, and then `Faces meant:` with each line of the scene's
`faces` under its person's label (the module's `LABELS`, or the role itself), the viewer's only in a `plain` picture; nothing
else of the scene. The task says that an ordinary picture shows the same moment from a camera outside, with everyone,
the viewer included, seen from outside, and that its first-person fields (`wholeViewer`, `firstPerson`,
`bodyPlausible`, `reflection`) are `na`; its `people` counts the viewer and its `matches` covers them. Beside the
questions it was asked before and `matches`, it answers for every picture `expression` (each person of `Faces meant`
it shows, by label: `yes`, `partly`, `no` or `hidden`), `stoic` (`yes`/`no`), `marks` (`yes`/`no`/`na`: the flush,
sweat or water the faces line names) and `anatomy` (`ok`/`minor`/`major`). The task is the same for every arm: nothing
in it tells a `noref` picture apart. `tally` counts by writer, arm and mode, each by case, over the `people-off` cells,
and in all, with the faces' counts in every row; it does not read `matches` of a `noref` picture, which had no
portrait to match. Its `references-noref` rows set the `claude` pictures with references beside the `noref` pictures
of the same scenes, by checkpoint and mode, and its `kitchen-attention` row sets every settled `klein-kitchen`
picture beside its `klein` one: how many were drawn, the verdicts and the codes refused, the milliseconds of both
(median, mean, max) and the judge's fields of both over the frames judged on both sides alone, with the count of pairs
left out. Of the judge's answers only the values the task allows are counted: anything else, free text included, is
counted as `other`, and never printed. The owner's blind page has one question for a scene at a seed in one mode,
with every writer's pictures of it under letters: six, Qwen and Klein of each writer, eight with Gemma's two pose
pictures, and two more at seed 7, the `noref` ones, ten at most (`blind-review.ts` deals up to twelve);
`klein-kitchen` is not on it. Its pairs are a small blind page of their own, «Klein: внимание kitchen или без», one
question a frame with its two pictures, with the same two picks. Its text is the
scene written for the stand and the scene Gemma told, as «Текст 1» and «Текст 2» in an order drawn once for each
question; which is which is in `review/texts.key.json`, which the page is never given, and the texts are compared on
the same scene. Built
with `--faces`, it asks two picks of every question: the picture that fits the scene best, and «где лица живее».
`score` counts both by writer and arm, the second as `faces`. `review/gallery-after-scoring.html` names every arm,
scene by scene with a row per way, and is opened only after the page is scored.

`judge` starts no session for a bundle that has a report on its present map, unless `--again`, and hands each
session its task on stdin (`-` as the prompt, which `codex exec` reads from there), so that no process's arguments
hold it.

**What stays as it is, and why.**
- The judge's copy of a bundle stays in the system's temporary directory: under `$OUT` it would lie two levels below
  `keys.json` and the files named by arm, and a judge that ran `ls ../..` would see them.
- A session's events and stderr stay in `$OUT/judge/`, the stand's private directory, beside its report.
- The owner's page keeps its answers in the browser's storage until they are downloaded: it is the owner's own
  browser, and it holds the picks alone.
- The picture card's destroy stays a step the lead runs (step 5); the renter's guard destroys the card at its
  destroyBy in any case, whatever the stand did.
